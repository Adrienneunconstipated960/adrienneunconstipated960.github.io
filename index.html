CASE 01 / SYSTEMS ENGINEERING · NASA MARSHALL

Robotic-arm workspace and verification

Requirements assessment

Could a legacy robotic arm support lunar-dust mitigation tests inside a constrained high-vacuum chamber? I used ROS2 for arm control and calibration and Thermal Desktop for high-vacuum thermal modeling as part of that same NASA project, alongside workspace analysis, actuator constraints, and verification planning.

CONTROL + CALIBRATION ROS2

Used to control and calibrate the robotic arm for the project's motion work.

HIGH-VACUUM THERMAL MODEL Thermal Desktop

Used to model the arm's thermal behavior for a high-vacuum environment.

Engineering approach
  • Control and calibrate the robotic arm using ROS2.
  • Model the arm's high-vacuum thermal behavior using Thermal Desktop.
  • Evaluate workspace and reach against chamber boundaries, target surfaces, and actuator/joint constraints.
  • Organize requirements and proposed tests to identify what must be verified before hardware use.
What the record supports

An assessment structured around 34 system requirements and six planned test scenarios, with a 10⁻⁷ torr target as an operating constraint. These figures describe evaluation scope, not six completed qualification tests.

Boundary: Control/calibration and thermal modeling work formed part of this assessment, not evidence of vacuum qualification, validated thermal margins, deployment, or six completed scenarios.

CASE 02 / SENIOR CAPSTONE · SYSTEMS INTEGRATION

IVerify: from tubing force to a state indication

Bench-level proof of concept

As project lead and systems integrator on a four-person team, I coordinated an external, clip-on concept intended to flag potential downstream restrictions in gravity-fed IV tubing without contacting the sterile fluid path.

System architecture

A clamp applies controlled preload. Tubing reaction force becomes a sensor signal, which is conditioned and compared with thresholds. The microcontroller supervises timing and controls the indication.

Reported prototype observations
  • Clamp preload averaged about 5.0 N, within approximately ±10% of the target in repeated bench trials.
  • Sensor output changed as test conditions changed; comparator thresholds and firmware-driven two-LED states were exercised.
  • Selected tubing showed no visible puncture, leakage, or permanent collapse in the reported tests.
Preload≈5.0 NReported average, bench trials
Variation≈±10%Relative to target preload
Indication2 LEDsDemonstrated prototype, not final UI

Boundary: A student prototype, not a clinically validated or approved medical device. Quantified flow sensitivity, false-alarm rates, and repeatable detection-time distributions still need testing. Three-color indication was a design goal, not implemented in the reported prototype.

CASE 03 / EE370L · INSTRUMENTATION LAB

Strain-gauge calibration and vibration response

Recorded bench observations

In partner-based lab work, we calibrated a strain-gauge amplifier and observed the response of a metal strip under mechanical excitation. Signal quality mattered as much as obtaining a number.

Measurement method
  • Set up a half-bridge strain gauge and calibrated the signal-conditioning indicator before measurement.
  • Compare loaded and unloaded readings, then observe a mechanically excited waveform on an oscilloscope.
  • Discard the first noisy trace for interpretation and use the cleaner strummed trace reported in the lab.
Instrumentation

Strain-gauge half-bridge, Validyne CD12 transducer indicator, Keysight MSOX3000 oscilloscope, and Keithley 2110 multimeter.
