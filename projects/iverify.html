IVerify

A non-invasive IV occlusion detection concept

ON THIS PAGEMethods & toolsOutcomesEvidence & limitationsConcept CAD & design basis
PROJECT 09 / 16

As project lead and systems integrator on a four-person senior-design team, I coordinated development of a non-invasive clip-on concept for gravity-fed IV tubing. The system applies repeatable mechanical preload and senses the tube reaction force as an indirect indicator of restrictions. Analog filtering and threshold logic feed microcontroller supervision, translating measurements into understandable status indications.

My contribution / team context

Project lead and systems integrator for a four-person senior-design team; coordinated subsystem interfaces, system architecture, integration, and reporting.

Engineering challenge

Detect possible downstream flow restrictions without cutting, puncturing, or entering the sterile fluid pathway.

Methods and approach

Integrate a clamp, force sensor, analog front end, threshold detection, microcontroller state logic, and a visual interface. The product concept defined normal, partial-restriction, and occlusion states.

Tools and environment

Mechanical sensing, signal conditioning, threshold logic, embedded systems, risk analysis, verification planning.

Evidence and limitations

Bench-level student proof of concept only. Not a clinically validated or approved device. The prototype used two LEDs, not the final proposed three-color interface.

Documented outcome

A bench-level, non-clinical proof of concept using two indicator LEDs. The proposed full three-color interface and clinical performance still required further testing.

Inside the IVerify design concept

The student prototype and the later CAD reconstruction represent different stages of work. This section documents the openly available reconstructed geometry, intended sensing approach, and design targets without implying that the reconstructed parts were fabricated or clinically tested.

CONCEPT CAD · NINE SOLIDS

CAD files you can inspect

The public reconstruction provides an editable mechanical reference rather than a finished product. It separates a fixed jaw, removable jaw, reference tube, contact plunger, and placeholder sensor and indicator hardware.

  • 3.0 mm tubing used as an assumed reference size.
  • 46 × 30 × 18 mm combined jaw envelope, invented for the reconstruction.
  • Nine-solid STEP model and parametric CadQuery source.
  • No proven spring preload, fabricated latch, calibrated sensor, or tested tolerance stack-up.

How the proposed sensing path works

This is the functional architecture of the student concept, not a reproduced circuit diagram or a clinically established relationship between restriction and sensed force.

  1. 01 · CONTACTPreloaded tubingMechanical reaction at the clamp
  2. 02 · SENSEForce sensorExternal force into an electrical signal
  3. 03 · CONDITIONAnalog front endGain and filtering
  4. 04 · DECIDEThreshold + MCUCandidate events and timing logic
  5. 05 · INDICATELED statusTwo LEDs on the reported bench prototype

What is demonstrated versus proposed

Spring 2026 student prototype

Bench-level investigation of external tubing-force sensing, analog threshold response, microcontroller supervision, and two-LED indications. These results do not establish clinical accuracy or safety.

Later reconstructed model

Parametric, AI-assisted concept geometry informed by the public project description. The removable jaw and three-LED board are illustrative design changes, not recovered original CAD or proven hardware.

Evidence boundary: No quantitative clinical sensitivity, false-alarm rate, validated flow classification, or approved patient-use capability is claimed.
